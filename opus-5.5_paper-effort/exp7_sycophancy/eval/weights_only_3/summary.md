**Pair 100 evidence:** Signs fixed with a GELU-gate anchor validated across all 18 layers. (1) 14 heads, sharpest L10 h0/h2/h3, change attention to a fixed token ~11 back (likely the guess); A attends less, B more, robust across variants. (2) In an output-derived answer-token subspace, OV copying is weaker in A: copy heads copy less (L15h2, L14h2, L7h0/2/3, L10h0/2) and anti-copy heads suppress more; about -2 sigma vs random subspaces, consistent in 20 of 21 constructions. (3) Strong duplicate-token heads (L3h3, L0h1) match repeated answer tokens more in B; negative net in all 15 constructions. (4) L12 h0/h1/h3 and L13h3 have the largest query rotations and write answer-token directions. (5) L7 u256/u186 switch at the answer position. Main caveat: B's verdict assumes the ~11-back token is the guess. Counter-evidence: a crude isotropic full-space OV trace favoured A, and early heads L0h2/L2h1 match more in A.

**Pair 101 evidence:** Full fine-tune with Adam-uniform deltas, so magnitudes are uninformative. Scale-free tests (validated on base heads: copy heads 95-100% positive diagonals, anti-copy 1-5%) show D's OV/MLP copy changes are null (|z|<=1.4), while QK identity-matching changes are large and robust (z up to +/-13). Coordinated changes: in layer 15, A moves attention on the same keys from amplifiers H3/H0 to suppressor H1 (dB cos -0.25 vs base cos +0.39); in layer 9, guess-offset copy heads H0/H2 attend less to the end of the user turn in A. Copy-suppression heads L7H1/L8H0/L15H1 are stronger in A (z-weighted copy x QK statistic p~0.09). Final MLPs L16/L17: A pushes harder against the answer the residual already carries (combined z~3.4), assuming the base's pre-L17 residual leans toward the guess. Counter-evidence: L17H1 and L14H1 copy heads are more active in A, and magnitude-weighted attention statistics are null, so confidence is modest.

**Pair 102 evidence:** A-B is a dense full fine-tune difference. From layer 11 on, MLP and attention output changes share one answer-difference subspace (17-45% of their change vs about 3% for a random subspace); the largest logit-weighted writes are the L14-L17 MLPs. In that subspace, heads whose OV copies answer tokens (L15h0, L16h2, L8h2, L15h2, L17h1, L7h2/h3) gained prediction-to-token QK matching in A, while copy-suppression heads (L7h1, L9h1, L6h1, L10h3, L15h1) lost it: corr +0.29, sum +19.6, reached by 0/40 random and 1/20 structured control subspaces for the correlation (0/20 for the sum), robust to subspace construction, k, RoPE bands, gain removal and coordinate mapping. A therefore promotes answer tokens already present in the prompt; only the user's guess reliably is one, so A repeats the guess. Supported weakly by more mid-layer OV copying in A (z about 2); several other tests were null.
